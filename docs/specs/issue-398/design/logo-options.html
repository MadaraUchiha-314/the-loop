the-loop — logo options, round 2

Round 1 drew five loop structures with a wobbling, over-drawn hand; the review asked for character and less stress on the hand-drawn part. Round 2 keeps the hand — a flat brush held at an angle, pressed and lifted, never shaky — and gives each mark a personality: wit, strength, momentum, utility, calm. Each is one standalone SVG in one muted palette, shown on paper, on slate, at four sizes down to a favicon, and beside the name. Pick one on the ticket; feedback becomes a parameter change and a re-run.

option 1 · option-1-loop.svg

loop

wit

The name, written in one line, is itself the loops: the l's tall outer loop, the two inner loops of the o's, the p's loop below the line.

the-loop
  • One continuous brush stroke — the pen never lifts between the four letters, so the word is one loop made of loops.
  • Outer and inner are the letters' own: the l reaches above, the p reaches below, the two o's sit inside.
  • It is the wordmark and the mark at once; at favicon size it reads as a knotted line.

Drawn as: a Catmull-Rom spline through 45 hand-placed points, arc-length parameterised, drawn with a flat brush at 28°.

option 2 · option-2-knot.svg

Knot

strength

One line, three loops, six crossings — a trefoil knot: nothing holds a loop like another loop.

the-loop
  • Every loop passes over one neighbour and under the next; pull any one and the others hold it.
  • The centre is the inner loop no line draws — the space the three keep between them.
  • Drawn as rope, not as a diagram: the weave is what gives it weight.

Drawn as: the trefoil curve (sin t + 2 sin 2t, cos t − 2 cos 2t) as one brush stroke; the six crossings found numerically and woven with a mask.

option 3 · option-3-flick.svg

Flick

momentum

A stroke that loops back on itself twice and still moves forward: iteration with momentum.

the-loop
  • Each loop is a pass round the cycle; each is smaller than the last, because the work converges.
  • The outer sweep carries the inner loops — the line that goes round is the line that goes on.
  • It ends in an arrow, not a full stop: the loop is a direction.

Drawn as: a prolate cycloid (x = t − b·sin t, y = b·cos t) whose loop size b shrinks along t, swept at −24°, a brush that presses hardest through the loops, and a chevron on the tangent.

option 4 · option-4-clip.svg

Clip

utility, with a wink

A paperclip: one wire bent into an inner loop inside an outer loop — the thing that holds the work together.

the-loop
  • Everyone already knows what it does: it keeps the pages of one thing from coming apart.
  • Inner and outer are literal — the small loop sits inside the large one, and the same wire makes both.
  • Tilted like a clip left on a desk; drawn with a brush so it is an object, not an icon.

Drawn as: eight legs and arcs in the proportions of a wire clip, smoothed, tilted −22°, one brush stroke.

option 5 · option-5-enso.svg

Ensō

calm

Three brush circles, each left open where the hand lifted: the outer loop, the inner loop, and the one between — none of them closed until the work is.

the-loop
  • One breath per circle, heavy where it starts and dry where it ends; the gap is the loop still running.
  • Nested, not concentric in spirit: each opens at its own angle, each is its own colour.
  • The quiet option — a mark that reads at any size and asks nothing of the eye.

Drawn as: three arcs of 315°, each a brush that starts heavy and thins to a dry tail; ink, dusk and clay.
